Six checks

  1. Map resultsNeeds workYou're #6 in Google Maps for "furnace repair airdrie" and #9 for "furnace replacement near me". Your profile's main category is Plumber, which makes Google less sure you're the right pick for furnace searches.
  2. Google GuaranteedNeeds workThree shops sit in the Google Guaranteed box at the very top of both searches. Customers can call them in one tap without scrolling. You're not there yet.
  3. Search adsNeeds workGoogle's Ads Transparency Center shows no ads for your website in the last 30 days. Two shops advertise on "furnace repair airdrie" and three on "furnace replacement near me".
  4. ReviewsLooks good4.8 stars from 180 Google reviews. That's more reviews than two of the three shops above you on the map.
  5. Call and form trackingNeeds workYour site has Google Analytics, but nothing that counts phone calls. It doesn't matter much yet, but it has to be in place before any ad money is spent.
  6. Where clicks landNeeds workYour home page shows the phone number as plain text with no tap-to-call link, and there's no way to book online. On a phone, people have to copy the number to call.

What we'd do first

  1. Get into Google Guaranteed for furnace and plumbing calls

    It's the first thing people see on both searches, and you pay for each lead it sends, not each click. Your reviews would put you level with the shops there now.

  2. Run a small search campaign on furnace repair in Airdrie

    Google estimates about 170 searches a month for "furnace repair airdrie" alone, and only two shops advertise on it. A modest budget aimed at Airdrie would put you above the map.

  3. Set your main Google category to HVAC contractor

    Most of the searches above are about furnaces, and your profile leads with plumbing. Plumber can stay on as a second category.
