What we checked

  1. Budget and biddingNeeds workSearch - Plumbing runs out of its $45 a day budget often enough to miss about 41% of the searches it could show on. It's set to get as many conversions as possible, but most of what it counts are page visits, so it's partly aiming for the wrong thing.
  2. Searches you pay forNeeds workAbout $757 went to searches with nothing the account counts, including "plumber salary calgary" ($86), "plumber edmonton" ($78), and "how to unclog a drain" ($59). There are only 6 negative keywords, and 75% of keyword spend is on broad match, which lets Google match loosely.
  3. Call and form trackingNeeds workCalls from your ads are counted, 45 in 90 days, and 11 of 58 calls were missed. Form fills are tracked but not counted: 38 came in, and Google doesn't see them when it decides what to bid. Visits to your contact page are counted as conversions, which makes the numbers look better than they are.
  4. Location and scheduleNeeds workSearch - Plumbing and Performance Max show ads to people outside your area who search about it, and about $425 went to Edmonton, Red Deer, and Vancouver with no conversions. Ads run around the clock: 9 pm to 6 am cost $1,119 for 4 conversions, which may be worth it if you take night calls.
  5. Ads and extrasNeeds work4 of your 6 ads are rated poor or average by Google. You have sitelinks and a call button on the ads, but no callouts and no location, which shows your address and distance from the searcher.
  6. Where clicks landNeeds work1,650 clicks went to your home page, which scores 3 out of 10 for speed, and 29% of those clicks weren't on a mobile-friendly page. Your drain cleaning page does better, at 6 out of 10 and fully mobile-friendly.
  7. Google Guaranteed and the mapNeeds workWhen we searched, three shops sat in the Google Guaranteed box at the top of "plumber calgary" and two on "drain cleaning near me". You're in neither. You're second on the map for "drain cleaning near me" but #11 in Google Maps for "plumber calgary".

What I'd change, in order

  1. Count calls and form fills, not contact page visits

    64 of the 109 conversions are page visits, and 38 form fills aren't counted. Counting the real enquiries gives Google the right thing to aim for.

    Part of monthly managementWeek 1

  2. Block the searches that never turn into work

    About $757 went to searches like job hunting, do-it-yourself questions, and other cities. Adding them as negative keywords stops paying for them.

    Part of monthly managementWeek 1

  3. Show the ads only to people in your service area

    Switching both campaigns to people in your area, and turning off search partners, keeps the about $425 that went to Edmonton, Red Deer, and Vancouver.

    Part of monthly managementWeek 1

  4. Move the saved budget to Search - Plumbing

    It misses about 41% of the searches it could show on because the budget runs out, and it's where "plumber calgary" and "emergency plumber calgary" bring most of your calls.

    Part of monthly managementFirst two weeks

  5. Put a tap-to-call button at the top of your home page

    Most clicks land on the home page, which loads slowly on phones. A call button near the top and a lighter page would turn more of those visits into calls.

    Page fixes add-on, $500. Or your web person can do itFirst two weeks

  6. Apply for Google Guaranteed

    Five spots on your two main searches go to shops in the Google Guaranteed box, above the ads. If you qualify, you pay for each enquiry rather than each click.

    Part of monthly managementFirst month

If we ran it: the first month

  • Week 1: fix what's counted as a conversion, add the negative keywords, and tighten locations and search partners.
